EE 1 Evaluation Exam
Coverage: DC Circuits, AC Circuits, Circuit Transients and Polyphase

1. The specific resistance ρ depend upon
A. The area of cross section and the length of the conductor.
B. The material of the conductor, its area of the cross section and length.
C. The nature of the material of the conductor only.
D. The area of the cross-section of the conductor.
Answer: C
Solution: Resistivity (specific resistance) is a property of the material itself (and its temperature). R = ρL/A depends on length and area, but ρ does not.

2. The condition in Ohm’s law is that.
A. Ratio of V/I should be constant.
B. The temperature should remain constants
C. current should be proportional to voltage
D. the temperature should vary
Answer: B
Solution: Ohm's law (V ∝ I) holds only when physical conditions, chiefly temperature, remain constant.

3. Resistance of the conductor increases when
A. Its length increases
B. Both area and length increases
C. The area decreases
D. Specific resistance is kept constant
Answer: A
Solution: R = ρL/A, so R increases directly with length.
• Review note: choice C (area decreases) also increases R; the intended single answer is A.

4. The resistance of a conductor, when it temperature is increased.
A. Remain Constant
B. Increases
C. Varies
D. Decreases
Answer: B
Solution: Metallic conductors have a positive temperature coefficient: R₂ = R₁[1 + α(T₂ − T₁)], so R increases with temperature.

5. The resistance of the carbon when its filament temperature is decreased.
A. Increases
B. Decreases
C. Remains same
D. Increases enormously
Answer: A
Solution: Carbon has a negative temperature coefficient of resistance, so its resistance increases when its temperature is decreased.

6. Resistance of a rod does NOT depend on
A. The shape of its cross-sectional area
B. Its material
C. Its length
D. Its temperature
Answer: A
Solution: R = ρL/A depends on material (ρ), temperature, length and the size of the area, but not the shape of the cross-section.

7. Resistor R1 has twice the resistance of resistor R2. They are connected in parallel to a battery. Find the ratio of the thermal energy generation rate in resistor R1 to that of resistor R2.
A. 2:1
B. 1:4
C. 4:1
D. 1:2
Answer: D
Solution: In parallel both have the same voltage, so P = V²/R.
• P1/P2 = R2/R1 = R2/(2R2) = 1/2, i.e. 1:2

8. A resistor with resistance R1 and a resistor with resistance R2 are connected in parallel to an ideal battery with emf V. what is the rate of thermal energy generation in the resistor R1?
A. V²R1/(R1+R2)
B. V²/(R1 + R2)
C. V²/R2
D. V²/R1
Answer: D
Solution: In parallel, the full emf V appears across R1, so P1 = V²/R1.

9. A resistor with resistance R1 and a resistor with resistance R2 are connected in SERIES to an ideal battery with emf V. what is the rate of thermal energy generation in the resistor R1?
A. Twice that in resistor 2
B. Half that in resistor 2
C. Four times that in resistor 2
D. The same as that in resistor 2
Answer: A
Solution: In series the current is the same, so P = I²R and P1/P2 = R1/R2.
• With R1 = 2R2 (as in the preceding item), P1 = 2P2, twice that in resistor 2.
• Review note: the stem does not restate R1 = 2R2; the answer assumes it.

10. Two wires of the same material have the same length but different diameters. They are connected in series to a battery. Which of the following quantity is the same for the two wires?
A. Current
B. Current density
C. Voltage
D. End-to-end potential difference
Answer: A
Solution: Elements in series carry the same current. Current density (I/A) and voltage drop differ because the areas differ.

11. The current is zero in a conductor when NO potential difference is applied because ____.
A. The electrons are not moving fast enough.
B. Equal numbers of electrons and protons are moving together.
C. The electrons are not moving.
D. For every electron with a given velocity there is another with a velocity of equal magnitude and opposite direction.
Answer: D
Solution: Free electrons move randomly; with no applied field their velocities cancel on average, so there is no net drift and no current.

12. The direction of induced emf can be found by ____.
A. Len’z law
B. Fleming’s left hand rule
C. Fleming’s right hand rule
D. Kirchhoff’s law
Answer: C
Solution: Fleming's right-hand rule (generator rule) gives the direction of the emf induced in a conductor moving in a magnetic field.
• Review note: Lenz's law (A) also determines the direction of induced emf; C is the standard answer for this item.

13. Magnetic lines of force are Called
A. Hysteresis
B. Current Flux
C. Magnet
D. Motive Force
Answer: B
Solution: Magnetic lines of force collectively are called magnetic flux; choice B is the only one naming flux.

14. The law that induces emf and current always opposes the cause producing them was discovered by
A. Lenz
B. Faraday
C. Maxwell
D. Ohm
Answer: A
Solution: Lenz's law: the induced emf and current always oppose the change (cause) producing them.

15. Externally, magnetic line of force travel.
A. South to north
B. North to south
C. Negative to positive
D. Positive to negative
Answer: B
Solution: Outside a magnet, flux lines leave the north pole and enter the south pole; inside they return from south to north.

16. Three identical resistors are connected in delta across balance, 3-phase source. If one of the resistor is open,
A. Power will be double
B. Power is reducing by 33%
C. Power will become three times
D. Power will increase by 67%
Answer: B
Solution: Each delta resistor still has the full line voltage across it, so each takes P = V²/R.
• Before: 3P. After one opens: 2P.
• Reduction = (3P − 2P)/3P = 1/3 ≈ 33%

17. The magnetic potential in magnetic circuit can be measured in terms of.
A. Farads
B. coulombs
C. mmf
D. none of these
Answer: C
Solution: Magnetic potential (magnetic "voltage") is magnetomotive force, mmf = NI, in ampere-turns.

18. Permeance is analogous to.
A. Conductance
B. Resistance
C. Reluctance
D. none of these
Answer: A
Solution: Permeance is the reciprocal of reluctance, just as conductance is the reciprocal of resistance.

19. If the magnetic flux through a certain region is changing with time.
A. An emf exists around the boundary.
B. A current must flow around the boundary.
C. An electric field must exist at the boundary.
D. A magnetic field must exist at the boundary.
Answer: A
Solution: By Faraday's law, a changing flux induces an emf around any closed path bounding the region. A current flows only if a conducting path exists.

20. The tesla is a unit of _______.
A. Inductance
B. Capacitance
C. Magnetic Flux Density
D. Permittivity
Answer: C
Solution: 1 tesla = 1 Wb/m², the SI unit of magnetic flux density B.

21. Which statement about a charge placed on a dielectric material is true?
A. The charge diffuses across the material surface.
B. The charge diffuses through the interior of material.
C. The charge is confirmed to the region in which the charge was placed.
D. The charge increases the conductivity of material.
Answer: C
Solution: A dielectric (insulator) has no free charges to carry it away, so a charge placed on it stays (is confined) where it was placed.

22. The magnetic torque exerted on a flat current-carrying loop of wire by a uniform magnetic field B is _______.
A. Maximum when the plane of the loop is perpendicular to B.
B. Independent of the orientation of the loop.
C. Maximum when the plane of the loop is parallel to b
D. Dependent on the shape of the loop for a mixed loop area.
Answer: C
Solution: τ = NIAB sin θ, where θ is the angle between the loop's normal and B. Torque is maximum (θ = 90°) when the plane of the loop is parallel to B.

23. Whenever a conductor cuts magnetic flux, an emf is induced in that conductor. Above statement is due to
A. Joule’s Law
B. Faraday’s Law
C. Coulomb’s Law
D. Weber and Ewing’s Theory
Answer: B
Solution: This is Faraday's (first) law of electromagnetic induction.

24. On farad is …
A. coulomb –joule
B. 1 coulomb per volt
C. joule per volt
D. 1 volt per coulomb
Answer: B
Solution: C = Q/V, so 1 F = 1 C/V.

25. Which of the following will the effect of electric shock in the human body depends on?
A. Voltage flowing in the body
B. Line power
C. Current flowing in the body
D. Resistance in the line
Answer: C
Solution: The physiological effect of a shock depends on the current through the body (its magnitude, path and duration).

26. The coulombs force F, acts on two charges a distance r, apart. What is F proportional to?
A. r
B. r²
C. 1/r²
D. 1/r³
Answer: C
Solution: Coulomb's law: F = kq₁q₂/r², so F ∝ 1/r².

27. Current has a unit of
A. coulomb
B. ohm
C. coulomb per second
D. volt
Answer: C
Solution: I = Q/t, so 1 ampere = 1 coulomb per second.

28. Capacitors are said to.
A. Block AC and pass DC
B. Pass AC and DC
C. Block DC and pass AC
D. Block AC and DC
Answer: C
Solution: Xc = 1/(2πfC). For DC (f = 0) Xc is infinite (blocked); for AC Xc is finite (passed).

29. In a capacitor the electric charge is stored in.
A. metal plates
B. dielectric as well as metal plates
C. dielectric
D. casing
Answer: A
Solution: The charges +Q and −Q reside on the metal plates. (The energy is stored in the electric field in the dielectric.)

30. The ohm meter reading for a shorted capacitor is.
A. Infinity
B. Few Kilo Ohm
C. Few Mega Ohm
D. Zero
Answer: D
Solution: A shorted capacitor is a direct connection between its terminals, so the ohmmeter reads (about) zero.

31. For a parallel circuit consisting of resistance and reactance the value of power factor is the ratio of.
A. Impedance to reactance
B. Resistance to impedance
C. Reactance to impedance
D. Impedance to resistance
Answer: D
Solution: For parallel R and X: pf = G/Y = (1/R)/(1/Z) = Z/R, the ratio of impedance to resistance. (For a series circuit it is R/Z.)

32. In a series circuit the current is.
A. Constant
B. Proportional to the resistance
C. Zero always
D. Different in different resistors
Answer: A
Solution: There is only one path in a series circuit, so the same (constant) current flows through every element.

33. In parallel circuit the potential difference across the resistance.
A. Varies
B. is sometimes constant.
C. is deferent from the applied voltage.
D. is always constants.
Answer: D
Solution: Parallel branches are connected across the same two nodes, so each has the same (applied) voltage.

34. The current flowing in L and C at parallel resonant are.
A. Zero
B. Infinite
C. Equal
D. Different
Answer: C
Solution: At parallel resonance XL = XC, so the inductor and capacitor branch currents are equal in magnitude (and 180° apart).

35. Heating effect of current has undesirable side effect.
A. Electrical oven
B. Electric iron
C. Immersion heater
D. Vacuum cleaner
Answer: D
Solution: Ovens, irons and immersion heaters use the heating effect on purpose. In a vacuum cleaner (motor) the I²R heat is an unwanted loss.

36. In a balanced three phase star connected system, the line voltage is.
A. 0.707 times phase voltage.
B. the phasor sum of two phases voltages.
C. 1.414 times phase voltage.
D. the phasor differences of two phases voltages.
Answer: D
Solution: V_AB = V_AN − V_BN, the phasor difference of two phase voltages, with magnitude √3·V_phase.

37. In a star connected system, the line current is.
A. 0.707 time the phase current
B. equal to the phase current
C. 1.732 times of the phase current
D. 1.414 times the phase current
Answer: B
Solution: In a star (wye) connection each line is in series with one phase, so I_line = I_phase.

38. Two-wattmeter can be used to measure 3-phase power for a _____.
A. Balanced and unbalanced load only.
B. Balanced load only.
C. Unbalanced load only.
D. Unity power factor only.
Answer: A
Solution: By Blondel's theorem, two wattmeters measure total power of any 3-phase, 3-wire load, balanced or unbalanced.

39. In 2-wattmeter method, the reading in one of the wattmeter is zero when.
A. Power factor is unity.
B. Load in one of the phase is zero.
C. Pf is 0.5.
D. A neutral wire is not provided.
Answer: C
Solution: W1 = VL·IL·cos(30° − θ), W2 = VL·IL·cos(30° + θ). W2 = 0 when θ = 60°, i.e. pf = cos 60° = 0.5.

40. What characteristics of plates of the battery where ampere hour is rated?
A. Strength
B. Area
C. Distance
D. Thickness
Answer: B
Solution: The ampere-hour capacity of a battery depends on the amount of active material, i.e. the plate area.

41. The time constant is the time it takes the current in an inductive circuit to rise to
A. 66% of its final steady state value.
B. 70% of its final steady state value.
C. 63% of its final steady state value.
D. 50% of its final steady state value.
Answer: C
Solution: i = I(1 − e^(−t/τ)). At t = τ, i = (1 − e⁻¹)I = 0.632I ≈ 63%.

42. A capacitive C is charged through resistance R. the time constant of the charging circuit is given by
A. RC
B. 1/RC
C. C/R
D. R/C
Answer: A
Solution: For an RC circuit τ = RC (seconds).

43. At a dc steady state, capacitor looks like a/an ____ circuit and an inductor looks like a/an _____ circuit.
I. Grounded II. Short III. Open
A. I and II
B. III and II
C. II and III
D. III and III
Answer: B
Solution: At DC steady state a capacitor carries no current (open circuit, III) and an inductor has no voltage across it (short circuit, II).
• Note: the PDF prints the third item as "II. Open"; it is item III.

44. The time constant of an inductive circuit is defined as the ratio of
A. R/L
B. Lf/R
C. Rf/L
D. L/R
Answer: D
Solution: For an RL circuit τ = L/R (seconds).

45. A certain capacitor, in series with a resistor, is being charged. At the end of 10 ms its charge is half the final value. What is the time constant on this process in ms?
A. 2.3
B. 6.9
C. 10
D. 14
Answer: D
Solution: q = Q(1 − e^(−t/τ)) = 0.5Q, so e^(−t/τ) = 0.5
• t/τ = ln 2 = 0.693
• τ = 10 ms / 0.693 = 14.4 ms ≈ 14 ms

46. The greatest value attained during one half of the cycle is called the.
A. peak value
B. average value
C. rms value
D. effective value
Answer: A
Solution: The peak (maximum) value is the greatest instantaneous value reached in a half cycle.

47. For an RL circuit, the power factor cannot be less than ____ or greater than ____.
A. 0, 1
B. 0, -1
C. 1, 0
D. -1, 0
Answer: A
Solution: pf = cos θ with 0° ≤ θ ≤ 90° for an RL circuit, so 0 ≤ pf ≤ 1 (0 for pure L, 1 for pure R).

48. In an RLC circuits, the impedance at resonance is.
A. Maximum
B. Infinity
C. Minimum
D. Zero
Answer: C
Solution: At series resonance XL = XC, so Z = R, its minimum value.
• Note: this answer is for a series RLC circuit; a parallel RLC circuit has maximum impedance at resonance.

49. Which of the following AC device is used to measure the angle between the voltage and its current?
A. Reactive Meter
B. Power Factor Meter
C. Demand Meter
D. Phase Angle Meter
Answer: D
Solution: A phase angle meter reads the angle θ between voltage and current directly. A power factor meter reads cos θ.

50. A common utility’s tariff practice to penalize industrial consumer whose power factor fall below ____.
A. 80%, 90%
B. 75%, 90%
C. 75%, 75%
D. 75%, 85%
Answer: A
Solution: Utilities commonly penalize industrial consumers with low power factor (typically below 80–85%) and may give credit at high power factor (about 90% and above).

51. The reading of the wattmeter will be the same in the two wattmeter method when
A. load on one phase is zero
B. power factor is 0.5
C. power factor is unity
D. neutral is grounded
Answer: C
Solution: W1 = VL·IL·cos(30° − θ) and W2 = VL·IL·cos(30° + θ) are equal only when θ = 0°, i.e. unity power factor.

52. Find the resistivity of a conductor having 4 ohms resistance, 30 m length and 0.7 mm diameter.
A. 6.4 × 10⁻⁸ Ω-m
B. 7.73 × 10⁻⁸ Ω-m
C. 5.77 × 10⁻⁸ Ω-m
D. 5.13 × 10⁻⁸ Ω-m
Answer: D
Solution: A = πd²/4 = π(0.7 × 10⁻³)²/4 = 3.848 × 10⁻⁷ m²
• ρ = RA/L = 4 × 3.848 × 10⁻⁷ / 30 = 5.13 × 10⁻⁸ Ω-m

53. A 300 m copper conductor has a resistance of 250 ohms. What is the size of conductor?
A. 0.0162 mm
B. 1.62 mm
C. 0.162 mm
D. 16.2 mm
Answer: C
Solution: Using ρ(copper) = 1.72 × 10⁻⁸ Ω-m:
• A = ρL/R = 1.72 × 10⁻⁸ × 300 / 250 = 2.064 × 10⁻⁸ m²
• d = √(4A/π) = √(2.628 × 10⁻⁸) = 1.62 × 10⁻⁴ m = 0.162 mm

54. Three resistance of 8, 6 and 12 are connected in series and then in parallel. Find the effective resistance in series connection.
A. 15
B. 26
C. 20
D. 13
Answer: B
Solution: R_series = 8 + 6 + 12 = 26 Ω

55. A 3 ohms and 6 ohm resistor are connected in parallel and the combination in series with an 18 ohms resistor. Calculate the total resistance.
A. 10 ohms
B. 20 ohms
C. 5 ohms
D. 15 ohms
Answer: B
Solution: 3 ∥ 6 = (3 × 6)/(3 + 6) = 2 Ω
• R_T = 2 + 18 = 20 Ω

56. Two 30 ohms resistance are connected in series. Find the value of resistor R in ohms to be connected across one of the 30 ohms resistors so that the total resistance is 40 ohms.
A. 15
B. 30
C. 10
D. 25
Answer: A
Solution: 30 + (30R)/(30 + R) = 40, so (30R)/(30 + R) = 10
• 30R = 300 + 10R, R = 15 Ω

57. The resistance of a length of copper wire is 3.60 ohms at 20°C. What is the resistance in ohms at 80°C?
A. 4.45
B. 4.86
C. 4.34
D. 4.65
Answer: A
Solution: α(copper, 20°C) = 0.00393 /°C
• R80 = 3.60[1 + 0.00393(80 − 20)] = 3.60 × 1.2358 = 4.45 Ω

58. Five resistances of 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30 ohms are connected in parallel. Calculate the joint resistance.
A. 1.0
B. 3.64
C. 3.46
D. 100
Answer: C
Solution: 1/R = 1/10 + 1/15 + 1/20 + 1/25 + 1/30 = 0.1 + 0.0667 + 0.05 + 0.04 + 0.0333 = 0.29
• R = 1/0.29 = 3.45 Ω ≈ 3.46 Ω

59. A light bulb having a tungsten filament draws 0.5 Amp at 110 volts. The cold resistance of the filament is 20 ohms at 20°C with temperature coefficient of resistance for tungsten at this temperature of 0.005 per °C. Determine the operating temperature of the bulb.
A. 1220°C
B. 2520°C
C. 520°C
D. 2020°C
Answer: D
Solution: Hot resistance R = 110/0.5 = 220 Ω
• 220 = 20[1 + 0.005(T − 20)], so 0.005(T − 20) = 10
• T − 20 = 2000, T = 2020°C

60. A 5 kW load at 120 volts is to be supplied by a line 100 m long. What is the diameter of copper wire if the allowable voltage drop is 5%?
A. 3.57 mm
B. 5.38 mm
C. 4.87 mm
D. 6.82 mm
Answer: B
Solution: I = 5000/120 = 41.67 A; allowable drop = 0.05 × 120 = 6 V
• Line (go and return, 200 m) resistance R = 6/41.67 = 0.144 Ω
• A = ρL/R = 1.72 × 10⁻⁸ × 200 / 0.144 = 2.39 × 10⁻⁵ m², d = √(4A/π) ≈ 5.5 mm
• Closest choice: 5.38 mm (the exact figure depends on the resistivity value used).

63. A resistance of 4 ohms is connected across 100 V supply. When another “R” ohm is connected in parallel with 4 ohm, the total current from the supply was found to be 50 A. the value of the resistance “R” is.

64. The voltage applied across an electric press was reduced by 50%. The power consumed by the press will be reduced to
A. 25%
B. 60%
C. 75%
D. 50%
Answer: A
Solution: P = V²/R. With V' = 0.5V, P' = (0.5)²P = 0.25P, i.e. reduced to 25%.

66. Three 10-ohms resistance are connected in series across 120 Volt dc source. Find the value of resistor R in ohms to be connected across the two 10 ohms resistors so that the total current is 6 amperes.

67. Three resistances R1, R2 and R3 are connected in series across 120 V dc source. If R1 is 60 ohms, R2 has 40 volts with 0.5A current. Find R3 .

68. A wire 100 cm long and carrying current of 40 A is in perpendicular whose magnetic flux density is 1.5 Wb/m². Calculate the mechanical power which has velocity of 5 m/s.

71. Two identical coupled cols have an equivalent inductance of 80mH when connected series aiding, and 35 mH in series opposing. What is the value of the coefficient of coupling k?
A. 0.319
B. 0.391
C. 0.913
D. 0.139
Answer: B
Solution: L_aid − L_opp = 4M, so M = (80 − 35)/4 = 11.25 mH
• L_aid + L_opp = 4L (identical coils), so L = 115/4 = 28.75 mH
• k = M/√(L1L2) = 11.25/28.75 = 0.391

72. A loop of wire carrying a current of 2A is in shape of right triangle with two equal sides, each 15 cm long. A 0.7 T uniform field is in parallel to the hypotenuse. What is the resultant magnetic force on the two equal sides?
A. Zero
B. 0.41 N
C. 0.21 N
D. 0.31N
Answer: A
Solution: The net force on any closed loop in a uniform field is zero.
• The hypotenuse is parallel to B, so its force is zero.
• Therefore the resultant force on the two equal sides must also be zero (each is 2 × 0.15 × 0.7 × sin 45° = 0.148 N, in opposite directions).

73. When one coil of magnetically coupled pair has a current of 5 amperes, the resulting flux Φ11 = 0.2 mWb and flux Φ12 = 0.4 mWb respectively. If the turns are N1 =500 and N2=1500, find the mutual inductance, M.
A. 60 mH

C. 80mH
D. 100Mh

76. Find the distance of two identical charges of 4 μCoul when they experienced force of 250N.
A. 12 mm
B. 5 mm
C. 20 mm
D. 24 mm
